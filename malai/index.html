Why this bank

Islamic financing is compliant on paper.
It is broken in the customer's hands.

01

Opaque by default

Most customers cannot explain their own Murabaha, or say what the bank earns and why that isn't interest. Contracts are signed, not understood — and trust erodes with every unexplained line item.

02

Slow by design

Branch visits, wet signatures, days of waiting for a decision the bank's own data could give in seconds. Conventional digital lenders answer in minutes — but they're not an option for the Sharia-conscious.

03

Adversarial when it matters

Conventional lending monetises failure: late fees, penalty interest, collections pressure. Too many "Islamic" products copy the mechanics and translate the labels. The ethics live in servicing — or nowhere.

The structure, honestly

How Murabaha works — and why profit ≠ interest.

1
mal.ai buys the asset

The bank purchases the car from the dealer at SAR 85,000 and takes real ownership — and real risk. If it's damaged before sale, that's the bank's loss.

2
Cost + profit, disclosed

The bank offers the car to you at cost price plus a fixed, agreed profit: SAR 85,000 + SAR 13,600. Murabaha literally means "declared-profit sale".

3
You accept — the price is frozen

Once signed, SAR 98,600 is the debt. It can never grow — not through late payment, not through anything. Time doesn't make money here; a real sale did.

4
You pay in instalments

48 × SAR 2,054. Settle early and unearned profit is rebated (ibra'). Struggle, and the schedule defers with zero increase in what you owe.

The difference in one sentence

Interest charges you for time on money and grows if you slip. Murabaha profit is the margin on a real sale of a real asset, fixed on day one, incapable of growing after.

Basis: trade sale with disclosed markup · AAOIFI Sharia Standard No. 8 (Murabaha) · reviewed by the mal.ai Sharia Supervisory Board (concept)
After the signature

The promises that survive the signature.

Ibra' — settle early, pay less

Early settlement rebates the unearned profit. In the prototype, Sara settles month 18 of 48 and the agent waives SAR 5,100 of future profit — computed live, in the chat, before she confirms.

No late fees — a charity pledge

A late instalment triggers a small pledged donation to a registered charity — the AAOIFI-sanctioned deterrent — and none of it touches mal.ai's P&L. The bank cannot profit from your bad month.

Hardship without penalty

Lost income? The agent restructures: instalments defer, the tenor extends, and the total owed does not increase by a single halala. The debt was fixed the day you signed; hardship doesn't change that.
